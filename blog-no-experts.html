There Are No Experts

July 2, 2026 · by Sara Woerdehoff, Head of Marketing at WESCO Industries, Denison, Iowa

Let me save you some money: there are no experts.

There are people with receipts, and there are people with titles. Here are the ten most humiliating receipts in history, counted down.

10. Decca Records listened to the Beatles in 1962 and passed, explaining that guitar groups were on the way out. The Beatles became the biggest act in music history. Decca became the answer to a trivia question.

9. Time magazine announced in 1966 that shopping from home would flop, because women like to get out of the house and would never buy from a screen. Amazon delivers its regards, hourly.

8. Kodak invented the digital camera in 1975, looked at the future, and buried it in a drawer to protect film sales. Kodak went bankrupt in 2012, killed by the thing in the drawer.

7. Lord Kelvin, the greatest physicist alive, declared heavier than air flying machines impossible. Eight years later, two bicycle mechanics from Ohio were in the air.

6. Irving Fisher, the most respected economist in America, called the 1929 stock market a permanently high plateau. The plateau lasted about two weeks. Then it became a cliff.

5. A hedge fund staffed by Nobel Prize winning economists lost money so fast in 1998 that the Federal Reserve had to organize a rescue, to save the banks from the smartest men in finance.

4. Doctors posed in cigarette ads in their white coats. More doctors smoke Camels than any other cigarette, said the ads, and the profession cashed the checks while the patients cashed out.

3. Ignaz Semmelweis proved that doctors washing their hands stopped mothers from dying after childbirth. His colleagues were so offended by the suggestion that they destroyed his career and his mind, then went back to delivering babies with filthy hands.

2. In 1949, the Nobel Prize in Medicine, the highest honor in the field, went to the lobotomy. The experts did not just get it wrong. They gave it a medal.

1. Eugenics. Forced sterilization of the people deemed unfit, tens of thousands of Americans, taught at the greatest universities on earth and blessed by the Supreme Court. It was not ignorance. It was consensus, with diplomas.

Every entry arrived with credentials, confidence, and the agreement of every smart person in the room. The people who turned out right were nobodies with a test, a notebook, and the nerve to check.

Titles are costumes. Receipts are evidence. There are no experts.
