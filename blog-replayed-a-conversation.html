I Have Replayed a Conversation From 2019 Four Hundred Times and I Am Finally Winning It

September 21, 2026 · by Sara Woerdehoff, Head of Marketing at WESCO Industries, Denison, Iowa

In the interest of full disclosure, my brain did not come with an off switch, an idle setting, or adult supervision. Here is the factory documentation.

It never clocks out. At 3 a.m., while respectable brains are asleep, mine is working out how you would perform CPR on a giraffe. It has urgent questions that cannot wait for daylight. If a zombie bites a vampire, which one changes? Do caterpillars know about the wings in advance, or do they go to sleep and wake up to a complete surprise? Your fingers have fingertips. Your toes do not have toe tips, and yet you can tiptoe and cannot tipfinger. My brain found that inconsistency years ago and has refused to close the case. It also writes short fiction about strangers in public, complete with backstories and motives, and once it has cast someone as a villain there is no appeals process. Somewhere along the way the universe decided I would not be getting math, and honestly, given everything else in here, that was probably a safety call.

Every decision requires a full investigation. The smallest choice sends me down a rabbit hole to review the literature, compare options nobody asked about, and emerge forty minutes later with a position paper. Then, to actually get anything done, I narrate myself out loud like a nature documentary. Pizza in the oven. Pizza in the oven. I repeat things to pin them to the board. I lose the end of the sentence I am currently in the middle of. Ask me to elaborate on something I said thirty seconds ago and the honest answer is no, I cannot, it is gone. And yet I can tell you the exact location of an object I have not seen since spring, down to the drawer and what it is sitting on top of. The filing system is chaos. The retrieval is flawless. Nobody knows why. I would also like it noted that if anyone is hiring people to sort things by color, shape, and size, I am available and would consider it a promotion.

The body is run by a committee that does not meet. I forget to eat, then act surprised by the headache. Water is apparently optional in this system, filed under things we will get to eventually. The bathroom gets used as a reward for finishing a task, like I am negotiating with a hostage taker who is also me. Caffeine sharpens the focus right up until it puts me down for a nap, and the documentation on that is unclear. My reflexes, meanwhile, are elite and useless. I once nearly dropped my phone on soft carpet, panicked, and slapped it directly into a wall at speed. Lightning fast. Zero skill involved. The phone survived. My dignity filed a report.

Conversations are a contact sport. Put me in a room full of people talking and I will hear every conversation in it except the one I am actually in. Someone tells me their name and it evaporates on contact, because my attention has already moved on to reading everything else about them, and the name was honestly the least interesting data point available. If you tell me a story, I will either interrupt to tell mine, or concentrate so hard on not interrupting that I miss yours entirely. There is no third setting. Then I will replay the whole exchange for years afterward, like game film, usually at night. The drive home happens in total silence while I daydream the entire route. I arrive with no memory of the road and strong opinions about a scenario that does not exist. At night, on an unfamiliar road, the entire navigation strategy is hope. I hope this is the road. And if I am stuck behind a slow driver, I drift a little to the right, so the people behind me can see that it is not my fault.

Social settings run on a gauge. When I go quiet in a new group, I am not shy. I am measuring how weird I am allowed to be in this particular room, and calibration takes a minute. The exhausting part of socializing is not the people. It is the monitoring, running a full time broadcast of myself to make sure it is all landing normally. This is why the best part of a haircut is the blow drying, when conversation becomes physically impossible and everybody is free. The best time to call me is text message. Afterward I will recover by rewatching a series I have already seen a hundred times, because new television is homework and old television is a weighted blanket. If you ask about my dream job, the honest answer is not participating in society, just opting out entirely, preferably with snacks. I have also, more than once, laid next to someone and tried to match my breathing to theirs out of pure curiosity, and nearly died. Research is dangerous.

Spite is load bearing. Tell me to do something I was already about to do and it will now be done later, out of pure spite, possibly next quarter. My grudges are archival quality. I still do not like how everyone acted over toilet paper, and that file is not closing. I will not be letting it go. I will be taking it to the grave, thanks. As for my enemies, not one of them has a private jet. Low budget opposition. I choose to take that as a compliment to my screening process.

The pep talks are not gentle. When it all goes sideways, the voice in my head does not do affirmations. There is a video making the rounds of a loud little hype man shouting encouragement over a parking lot at night, swear words included, and I have never felt more accurately documented. That is the management style in here. Volume, urgency, mild profanity. Under his leadership the survival rate so far is a hundred percent.

None of this is a complaint. All of my character development has been involuntary, and somehow the factory settings keep getting the job done. The brain that cannot remember why it walked into the kitchen will also not release a problem until the problem apologizes. The narration, the rabbit holes, the game film, the spite, the 3 a.m. research department, all of it runs the same direction in the end. It took me years to figure it out, so I will save you the time.

All of these flaws are my superpower.
